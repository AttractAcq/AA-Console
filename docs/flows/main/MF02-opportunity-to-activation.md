# MF02 — Opportunity to activation

**Flow.** MF02 · main · Foundation
**Purpose.** Turn intelligence into a named micro-niche, prove it is worth attacking, give it
an awareness journey, and approve it so a campaign can be built from it.
**Scope.** Market Opportunities end to end. Nothing external.
**Owner.** Marketing Director.
**Last reviewed.** 25 October 2026.
**Entry.** E2 — MF01 is done.
**Exit.** An opportunity at status `approved` with an awareness journey and no readiness blocker.

**Composes:** SF13, SF14, SF15, SF16, SF17, SF18.

---

## The one rule

**An opportunity is the commercial reason a campaign should exist.** It sits between
intelligence and campaigns on purpose. Read those two in the other order and you get
attractive campaigns that are not rooted in anything somebody would actually buy.

The second rule, nearly as important: **confidence is evidence, not enthusiasm.** The ladder
is `hypothesis → desk_research → conversation → first_party → proven`, and it only moves when
something real happened.

---

## Stage 1 — Discover

**Clients → [client] → Delivery → Market Opportunities → Discover**

**Find opportunities** queues the opportunity discovery agent, which reads the intelligence
from MF01 and proposes micro-niches. Each arrives at status `discovered`.

A micro-niche is narrower than a service line. "Garage conversions" is a micro-niche;
"building work" is not. The test: can you write one ad that speaks to it and would feel wrong
shown to the rest of the market? If not, it is too broad.

You can also add one by hand when the client or a salesperson already knows it.

---

## Stage 2 — Evaluate

**Clients → [client] → Delivery → Market Opportunities → Evaluate**

1. **Score it** (SF14). Record the factors, not just the total — the factors are what a
   reviewer argues with. The score and band are shown together.
2. **Set confidence honestly** (SF15):

| Confidence | What it means |
|---|---|
| `hypothesis` | Somebody's idea. Nothing has been checked |
| `desk_research` | The market data supports it |
| `conversation` | A real prospect or the client said so out loud |
| `first_party` | Our own data from this client shows it |
| `proven` | It has produced cash |

3. Fill in the commercial fields the panel asks for: **Desired outcome**,
   **Purchase trigger**, **Conversion action**, positioning. These are what the campaign
   brief and the offer both read.
4. Add **proof requirements** — **Add requirement** — naming the proof this niche will
   demand before it believes anything. A worked example: *"A completed garage conversion with
   costs."* This is what MF06 goes and gets, and naming it now is what stops the campaign
   stalling later waiting for proof nobody asked for in time.

Reject the ones that do not survive. A rejected opportunity with a reason is more useful than
a long list of maybes.

---

## Stage 3 — The awareness journey

**Clients → [client] → Delivery → Market Opportunities → Strategize → Awareness journey**

This is the stage that makes the content work, and the one most often skipped.

For each awareness stage of this niche, record four things:

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | The awareness level, in your own words |
| **Mindset** | What they believe and feel at that point |
| **Message** | The one thing to say to move them one step |
| **Asset direction** | What the asset should be and do |
| **Distribution note** | Where this lands — profile, page, ad, post |

**Why it matters.** `opportunity_readiness` refuses to call the opportunity ready without it,
with the sentence *"No awareness journey, so there is nothing to brief content or proof from."*
Every brief in MF07 and MF08 points at one of these stages, and one ad answers one stage.

**The gap to know about (G06).** Nothing in the system maps an awareness stage to one of the
sixteen templates. You make that call yourself: a stage whose mindset is "does not know the
problem has a name" is P4 or P1 territory; "comparing providers" is R1 or O1; "ready, just
needs a reason now" is O1. Write the template you intend into the stage's distribution note
so the person building the campaign does not have to guess.

---

## Stage 4 — Readiness

**Clients → [client] → Delivery → Market Opportunities → Activate**

Read the readiness line. `opportunity_readiness` names what is missing in one sentence
(SF17) — awareness stages, proof requirements, scores, a conversion action. Clear each one.

---

## Stage 5 — Approve and create the campaign

Still on **Activate**:

1. **Approve** — the opportunity moves to `approved`. Only an approved opportunity can
   create a campaign.
2. **Choose an opportunity** → the approved one.
3. **Campaign name** → what it will be called in Meta too, so name it for a stranger reading
   an ad account.
4. Mode — **organic** or **paid**. *A campaign is one or the other, for its whole life.*
   For paid, also *choose Meta Ads or Google Ads.*
5. **Create and plan** — creates the campaign and queues the planner.

The campaign now carries the opportunity as its origin, readable in `campaign_origins`, so
the question "why does this campaign exist" has an answer that is a record rather than a
memory (SF18).

Status moves on as the work does: `approved` → `active` → `learning` → `scaling`, or
`paused`. Those are states you set from evidence, in MF13.

---

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Read this |
|---|---|
| Discovery returns nothing useful | The intelligence is thin. Go back to MF01 stage 4 and read the records |
| **Approve** is refused | Readiness has an unmet item. The sentence names it (SF17) |
| Cannot create a campaign | The opportunity is not `approved`, or no mode/platform was chosen |
| Campaign created with no plan | The planner job is queued or held (SF53) |
