# SF01 — Onboard a client

**Where.** Clients → [client] → Account → Onboarding
**Owner.** Client manager. **Reviewed.** 25 October 2026. **Part of** MF01.

## Why it matters
Every agent downstream reads this. A thin business overview produces plausible, generic
output that looks fine and converts nobody.

## Do it
Fill from the sales call and the client's own words, not their website.

| Field | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| Primary contact / Their role | The person who actually answers. "Owner, practice manager…" |
| WhatsApp / Website / Instagram / Facebook | As they are, not as they should be |
| **Address** | Drives the Google Business Profile work (SF03) |
| **Business overview** | What they do, for whom, and what makes it what it is |
| **Ideal customer** | Their words. The ICP agent refines; it does not invent |
| **Main offer** | What they sell and roughly for what |
| Competitors | Who they lose to, named |
| Brand voice | How they talk. The copywriter reads this |
| Proof / testimonials | What proof exists, even informally — seeds MF06 |
| Current marketing | So we do not duplicate or contradict it |
| Sales process | What happens after a lead arrives — shapes MF12 |
| Primary / Secondary colour | Seeds the brand profile |

## What each unlocks
Business overview and ideal customer → the whole intelligence pass (SF07). Address → Google
Profile. Proof notes → the first proof capture. Sales process → how MF12 is set up.

## Gotcha
Onboarding is a record, not a form to get past. Returning to it after the first intelligence
pass and correcting it is normal and improves everything built later.
