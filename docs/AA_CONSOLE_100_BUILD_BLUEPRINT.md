## 1. The 100/100 definition

AA Console serves multiple service businesses, each isolated from the others. For each client it should ingest a real business context, connected accounts and genuine proof; identify specific buying opportunities; bind a signed offer; choose an organic or paid campaign route; create and quality-check content, pages, ads and conversion assets; submit every external action to one Approval Inbox for a person's decision on the **exact version, destination, scope and consequence**; execute approved actions once; capture leads and conversations; nurture and book them; record sales and cash; attribute outcomes with honest uncertainty; and use observed results to improve subsequent plans. Scheduled and internal work should run without a person repeatedly pressing buttons. This is the meaning of **automatic** in this document. The single required human touch at an external boundary is deliberate and was explicitly confirmed by Alex on 29 October 2026.

The supplied `MF12` sales SOP says that a human closes a service sale. The system must automatically prepare, qualify, route, book, remind, hand over, capture the result from an authorized CRM/payment/meeting source where available, and follow up under approval; it must never invent a sale or cash receipt. For businesses with a legitimate self-service checkout, the same acquisition spine may connect an approved checkout and observe payment. A human commercial conversation and a customer's choice are real-world events, not failures of software autonomy.

An external action includes an organic or proof post, a profile change or reply, a page or sales-agent deployment, a lead message, a magnet delivery, creating paused ad objects, activating ads, and changing spend. **Every one** must appear in the unified Approval Inbox before execution. The same Inbox must show **all other human decisions**, including client integration grants and activation switches, opportunity activation, offer signoff, proof rights/acceptance, creative acceptance, recruitment release and production code/schema/runtime releases. Each card identifies its decision type and owning authority; the Inbox records release approval and links the PR/SHA while `AGENTS.md` still governs who may merge or deploy. Approval is immutable and binds the actual payload bytes or canonical hash, artifact and campaign versions, destination/account, action, budget or send scope, and reviewer. Changed inputs make the approval stale. The executor must re-check current rights, consent, credentials, account identity, switches and approval immediately before a write. Record an external ID/URL, response and attempt outcome; ambiguous results must be reconciled rather than blindly retried.

For one-to-one messages and magnet sends, a standing sequence or template approval is **preparation**, not permission to send an unspecified message to an unspecified person. The Inbox must show the exact rendered body, channel, recipient identity or a frozen recipient list, timing and consent basis. A reviewer may approve an immutable batch whose member payload/recipient hashes are all inspectable; changing a member requires a new decision. This preserves Alex's exact-version rule while allowing scheduled execution after approval.

The target is not a promise of zero outages or attribution certainty. **100/100 means the pre-build release gate `R000` and every numbered build row below have their stated acceptance evidence, the cross-route scenarios in §6 pass on real or explicitly named test accounts, and unresolved external prerequisites are visible as blockers.** A capability that only renders, has a database row, or passes mocked tests is not credited as live.

### 1.1 Boundaries and route rules

1. `client_campaigns` is the operational campaign identity. The older `campaigns` table is a spend/reporting identity. Bridge them explicitly; never treat them as the same row.
2. A campaign has one immutable acquisition mode: **organic** or **paid**. Paid has one platform route, Meta or Google. Reuse on another route makes a new campaign with lineage and fresh approvals, activation and results.
3. A campaign package records versions of intelligence, opportunity, offer, proof, creative, page, magnet, sales path, destination and approval. Parent/child revisions retain history; no prompt-only reference counts as lineage.
4. Synthetic or unlicensed testimonials never become proof. Rights expiry or withdrawal blocks pending actions and creates a live-placement remediation task.
5. Every result is one of **observed**, **estimated** or **unknown**. Publishing is not a lead, a lead is not a booking, and a booking is not revenue.
6. Unsupported platform or account states fail closed with a visible blocker. A database `supported` flag may be true only when the deployed runtime has the matching handler and required credentials are configured for that client.
7. Preserve historical v1 in its merged archive, v5 deep-link compatibility and historical versions while using the canonical pages after PR #234. Most Delivery pages now serve their former v2 directly; Organic Content Distribution is the documented exception, with its existing page and Organic Calendar tab. Preserve client and employee access and recruitment separation. Do not reactivate archived v1 as a parallel live route.

### 1.4 Business journey encoded by the supplied manual flows

The user supplied 16 primary SOPs (`MF01`–`MF16`) and 82 secondary SOPs (`SF01`–`SF82`) from `docs/flows/`. They describe **how a person currently operates the service**. They are coverage evidence for this automated build, not implementation instructions. The target removes repeated clicking, polling, copying and manual handoffs wherever a machine can do them, while keeping the exact-version human approval and genuine human service interactions. §7 maps every SOP ID to build rows, including manual exceptions that must become visible states.

Audience states are `S0` stranger, `S1` watcher, `S2` owned/follower, `S3` identified lead, `S4` in conversation, `S5` customer and `S6` lapsed. Attract, nurture and convert are functions rather than a mandatory linear path: some campaigns move `S0` directly to `S3`, while a longer path buys a follower pool, nurtures it through organic content and proof, then converts it. The flagship `MF10` journey uses two separate campaigns: paid `P1` to build the pool, then `R1` or `O1` to convert it. The standing nurture layer between them is client-level organic content, proof and profile presence, not a second mode inside one campaign.

## 5. Release and operations controls

No row that changes a real destination may be closed from a mock test. A new external adapter must first operate against a controlled test account, then one internal AA account, then a second distinct service client. The gate record must name the exact account, permission scope, payload hash, human reviewer and observed external result. External “maybe succeeded” responses enter reconciliation and never an automatic retry that could duplicate a post, message, page or ad object. The operator can pause each client, destination and runtime; the UI shows which switch or missing dependency blocks work. Failure states do not mark campaigns live or assets delivered.

The release sequence for every schema/runtime/UI slice is: migration dry run and security review; realistic database and connector tests; staging migration; staged runtime and browser with matching schema; negative tests for cross-client, revoked rights, changed hashes, expired tokens and duplicate events; prepared PR; authorized merge/migration/deploy; post-release smoke with deployed SHA and flags; rollback rehearsal or documented recovery. The [root README](https://github.com/AttractAcq/AA-Console/blob/main/README.md), [runtime README](https://github.com/AttractAcq/AA-Console/blob/main/agent-runtime/README.md) and [gateway README](https://github.com/AttractAcq/AA-Console/blob/main/aa-mcp-gateway/README.md) describe the service boundaries. Never infer that a frontend deploy also deployed the runtime, gateway or database.

### 7.4 Flow-specific interpretation rules

- `MF10` is the flagship stranger-to-customer journey. `P1` pool building and a later `R1`/`O1` conversion are **separate campaigns**; the client-level organic/proof/profile nurture layer connects them by audience and attribution, not by changing one campaign's acquisition mode.
- `MF12` requires a real human closer for a high-touch service sale. The system owns booking, reminders, prepared handoff, post-call evidence capture and follow-up; a sale or cash receipt is recorded only from an authorized person or external source.
- `MF13` and `SF72` require observed, estimated and unknown to remain distinct through every dashboard, report and learning decision. Missing coverage cannot silently become zero.
- `MF14` is AA recruitment. Its page, applicant data, ads, attribution and team workflow must stay separate from the service-client lead and revenue spine.
- `MF16` and `SF82` require provider read-back before a campaign is called stopped. Archiving a database row is not evidence that spend, messages, widgets or pages have ceased.
- `SF12` is implemented by `A36` in this snapshot's source-audit notation: reviewed intelligence/strategy PDF and ZIP exports must be versioned and client-scoped, and any client-facing send requires its own exact-file approval.
- `SF70` is implemented by the expanded `A13`: deduplication needs a reviewed merge/split path for shared emails and changed contact details, retaining submissions and consent history.
